Ariadne

Privacy Policy

Effective 28 September 2026 · for Ariadne 0.2 and later

Ariadne collects nothing. Your journal is written to your own Mac and stays there.

There is no account to create, no server of ours to sign in to, and no analytics. Unless you turn on iCloud sync, the app makes no network requests at all — not one, not even to load its fonts. With sync on, it talks to exactly one place: your own iCloud.

What Ariadne stores

Everything you write — your entries, their dates, your threads and their colours, and your appearance settings — is saved in Ariadne's own private storage on your Mac.

That storage belongs to the app and to your user account on your computer. Nothing in it is transmitted anywhere unless you turn on iCloud sync, described below.

What Ariadne sends

With iCloud sync off — how every install starts — nothing. Ariadne's fonts, styles and code are bundled inside the app, so it works identically with your Mac disconnected from the internet.

There is no telemetry, no crash reporting, no usage analytics, no advertising, and no third-party software development kits. That stays true with sync on.

iCloud sync, if you turn it on

Sync is off until you switch it on in Settings, and everything in this section applies only after you do.

Sync exists so the same journal can follow you between your own devices. It uses Apple's iCloud — the account already on your Mac — so there is still no Ariadne account, and your writing never touches a server of ours; we could not read it even in transit, because it never passes through us.

Your entries, threads and appearance settings are stored in your iCloud's private database, encrypted by Apple's CloudKit encrypted-fields mechanism with keys that travel in your iCloud Keychain, between your devices. Apple hosts the ciphertext; the keys stay yours.

What iCloud can see: the contents of your entries and threads are encrypted, but the labels they are filed under are not. Each day is stored under its date, so Apple can tell which days you wrote, though not a word of what you wrote. Each thread is stored under a one-way hash of its name — not the name itself, but a short common name could be guessed by testing candidates against it. Nothing else about your journal is exposed: not its text, not your titles, not your colours.

Turning sync off stops all of it, immediately: the app goes back to making no network requests, and your journal remains, whole, on the Mac in front of you. Removing Ariadne's data from iCloud (in System Settings) removes the synced copy without touching what is written on any of your Macs.

No account, no profile

Ariadne never asks for your name, your email address, or any other identifying detail, because it has nothing to do with them. There is no profile associated with your use of the app. iCloud sync uses the Apple ID already signed into your Mac; Ariadne never sees its password and creates nothing new.

Exporting your journal

Ariadne can export your entire journal as a single file. That file is created by you, saved where you choose, and never sent anywhere by the app.

Once it leaves Ariadne, it is an ordinary document on your Mac. If you put it in a shared folder or a cloud drive, that service's privacy terms apply to it — not this policy.

Deleting your data

Delete individual entries inside Ariadne, or remove the app to remove its storage along with it. With sync off there is no server copy at all. With sync on, the only copy beyond your devices is in your own iCloud, and System Settings can delete it — there is still no one to ask but yourself.

Export first if you want to keep your writing. Deleting the app deletes the journal.

Children

Ariadne is suitable for all ages. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children under 13.

What this policy used to promise

Earlier versions of this policy said that if syncing ever shipped, it would be optional, encrypted so entries are unreadable on the server, and described here before it became available. Sync shipped in 0.2 on those terms — with one improvement on the promise: instead of an account you create, it uses the iCloud account you already have. The 28 September 2026 revision adds no new data collection; it spells out the filing metadata described above, which was true of sync from the day it shipped.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the effective date at the top will change with it, and the revised policy will be published here before the change takes effect in the app.
